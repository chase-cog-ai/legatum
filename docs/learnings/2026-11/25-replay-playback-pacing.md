## 2026-11-25: Replay render time sets the playback ceiling

- The original timer waited a full interval before asking the worker for the next frame. On the 156-turn Sparta game, a late-game sample spent roughly 4–6 ms per frame in WASM (including JSON serialization), under 1 ms parsing, and 85–211 ms across map paints even at the supported speeds. Worker post-to-receive time includes browser scheduling: it was typically near 1 ms at slow speeds but rose under fast playback.
- Prefetch one turn while the current turn is visible, and schedule from the previous deadline rather than adding preparation time to every interval. If a frame is late, leave time to see it instead of skipping turns to catch up. At 1440×900 the experimental 6× target of 200 ms measured about 255 ms/turn, so cap at 4×.
- In headless Chromium, playing from turn 131 through turn 155 gave median intervals of 1200 / 600 / 300 ms (1× / 2× / 4×, rounded) at 1000×600 and 1200 / 600 / 301 ms at 1440×900, with no turns skipped. Add `replayProfile` to the URL query to record `replay-worker` and `replay-draw` entries. The worker mark records WASM time, JSON parse time, absolute send and receive times, and turn; the draw measure records each canvas paint, including retries while terrain fills.
